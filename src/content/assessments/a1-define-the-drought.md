## What you are producing

An operative definition of a renewable-energy drought for a system you
specify. Not an essay about droughts — a test. Someone else must be able to
take your definition, apply it to a generation trace, and get the same answer
you did.

## How the canonical definition constrains this

The course ships one at [the event](/the-event/): 12% of combined installed
capacity, a 48-hour span, a 3-hour recovery allowance, wind and solar, winter
half-year. That is your starting point and your target. You are asked to move
at least one parameter and defend the move.

You are not asked to agree with it. You are asked to do better, for a system
you have named, and to show your working.

## What this feeds

Everything. [Site Against Failure](/assessments/a2-site-against-failure/) tests
a portfolio against *your* event, not the course's. If your definition admits
no events, you will have nothing to site against — which is itself a finding,
and one you should discover here rather than in week 8.

## What counts as a defensible answer

A boundary with consequences you can state. "I chose 15% because it admits the
three periods in my trace where the system would actually have needed help, and
excludes the eleven where it would not" is an argument. "I chose 15% because it
seemed reasonable" is not.

The strongest submissions say what their definition is blind to.
