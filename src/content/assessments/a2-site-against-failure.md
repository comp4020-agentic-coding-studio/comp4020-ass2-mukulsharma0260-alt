## What you are producing

A generation portfolio — what technology, how much, and where — together with
its correlation profile during your event. The portfolio is the artefact; the
correlation profile is the argument.

## What earlier work this uses

Your A1 definition, unchanged. If you revise it here, say so and say why; a
moving definition is allowed, a silent one is not.

## How the canonical definition constrains this

The event is a *simultaneous* low across wind and solar. That is what makes
siting hard: a portfolio optimised for annual yield tends to concentrate where
the resource is best, and the resource is best in places whose weather often
arrives together. Weeks 3 to 6 are the machinery for this — correlation,
spatial extent, depth below cut-in, and the hours when the wind–solar
complement stops working.

## What this feeds

[The 72-Hour Dispatch](/assessments/a3-the-72-hour-dispatch/) operates the
system you build here. A portfolio that looks strong on paper and collapses in
hour 40 of dispatch is a more useful submission than one that is never tested.

## What counts as a defensible answer

Evidence that you sited against a failure mode instead of against a mean. The
question is not "how much does this generate?" but "when this portfolio is
short, is it all short at once?"
