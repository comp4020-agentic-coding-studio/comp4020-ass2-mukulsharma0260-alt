## What you are producing

A system — generation, storage, reserve, and the operating rules that go with
it — plus two statements. The event it survives. The event that beats it.

The second statement carries the most weight in this course.

## What earlier work this uses

All of it. The definition from A1, the portfolio from A2, the dispatch log and
counterfactual from A3. This is the rung where the chain has to hold together:
if your dispatch exposed a weakness in your siting, the final design should
show that you noticed.

## How the canonical definition constrains this

Your declared failure boundary has to be expressed in the same vocabulary as
your event — a threshold, a duration, a recovery rule, a scope. "A worse
event" is not a boundary. "An event of the same depth lasting 96 hours, or one
of 60 hours arriving with storage already at 30%" is.

## What counts as a defensible answer

A design whose limits are stated as precisely as its capabilities. There is
deliberately no correct system here, and a submission claiming to survive
everything has either misunderstood the course or is not telling you about the
event it cannot survive.

Naming what defeats your design is not a weakness in the submission. It is the
submission.
