Spreading generation out is the standard answer to correlation, and it is a
good answer with a precise limit: it works when the system is bigger than the
weather, and stops working when it is not.

## How this complicates the thesis

Week 3 made correlation the enemy and diversification the obvious remedy. Week
4 takes the remedy back apart. A weather pattern that covers the whole
interconnected area is not diversified away by moving turbines around inside
it — and the transmission needed to reach genuinely uncorrelated weather is
itself a design cost, not a free assumption.

## In the session

We draw one anticyclone over a transmission map and grow the portfolio
underneath it in four steps: one farm, several nearby, widely separated, then
interconnected regions. You watch the correlation fall, and watch where it
stops falling.

## Its relationship to the canonical event

[The event](/the-event/) is defined for one synchronous area, modelled as a
single node. That is a deliberate simplification, and this is the week that
shows you what it costs.
