Wind and solar are genuinely anti-correlated across a year. The phrase that
matters in this course is "across a year".

## How this challenges the thesis

This is the week most likely to overturn something you believe. Complementarity
is real and widely relied upon, and the event is precisely the condition under
which it stops holding: the weather that stills the wind is often the weather
that puts cloud over the panels, in the season with the least daylight to lose.
A portfolio justified by average complementarity can be undefended exactly when
it matters.

## In the session

We push three portfolios — wind-only, solar-only, mixed — through the same
event and ask a sharper question than "which is better". Does the mix remove
the failure, shorten it, or simply move it to a different hour?

## Its relationship to the canonical event

[The event](/the-event/) counts wind and solar *combined*, and week 6 is why.
A definition that watched either source alone would miss the condition that
actually threatens the system.
