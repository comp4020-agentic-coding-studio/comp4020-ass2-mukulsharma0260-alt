Every capacity metric rewards a machine for running. Reserve generation exists
to do the opposite, and it is judged by an availability it is almost never
asked to demonstrate.

## How this challenges the thesis

The thesis is about engineering defensibility, and this week points out that
the last line of defence is bought with money rather than designed with
physics. An asset that sits idle for a year is, by the usual metrics, a failure
and, by the standard of the event, the reason the system survived. If you are
unwilling to pay for idleness, your declared survival is conditional on the
event not arriving.

## In the session

We look at a fictional reserve plant credited with 41 hours of operation in a
year and ask what it was worth. Then you put a number on reliability yourself:
what would you pay, per household per year, to keep it available?

There is no correct figure. There are indefensible ones.
